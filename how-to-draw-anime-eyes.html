How to draw anime eyes step by step

Six stages, and the two mistakes everyone makes first.

The eye is the part people judge. Get it right and a rough drawing still reads as good; get it wrong and a careful drawing still looks off. It is also the fastest part of anime style to learn, because it is built from a small number of repeatable shapes.

The six stages

1. The upper lash line

Start with the thick curve across the top. This is the single most important line in the whole eye, and it is thicker than beginners expect — in most anime styles it is a solid dark band, not a thin outline. It curves down at the outer corner.

2. The lower line

Thin, often broken, and much shorter than the upper line. In many styles it does not reach the outer corner at all. Resist the urge to close the shape into a complete almond — that is what makes an eye look flat and generic.

3. The iris

A tall oval, not a circle. It is cropped by the upper lash line — the top of the iris disappears behind it. Leaving the whole iris visible is what makes a character look startled.

4. The pupil

A smaller oval inside the iris, usually dark and solid, placed slightly above centre.

5. The catchlight

One or two white highlights, left completely blank. This is the step that makes an eye look alive. Place the main catchlight on the opposite side from wherever the light is coming from the rest of the drawing, and keep it consistent between both eyes.

6. Shading and lashes

Shade the top of the iris darker, fading lighter towards the bottom. Add lashes last, as short tapered strokes that come off the upper line and curve outwards.

The two mistakes

Mistake one: the eyes do not match

The far eye in a three-quarter view is narrower than the near eye, and it is partially hidden by the bridge of the nose. Drawing both eyes identically is the single most common beginner error, and it makes the face look flat.

Mistake two: forgetting the gap

The space between the eyes is roughly one eye wide. Beginners almost always place them too close together. Measure it before you commit to the lash lines.

How to practise this properly

Draw the same eye twenty times on one sheet. Not twenty different eyes — the same one, over and over. By about the twelfth attempt your hand stops thinking about the curve and starts producing it. That is the point of the exercise, and it cannot be skipped.

Then draw a pair. Then draw a pair at a three-quarter angle. Then move on to the full face.

If your lines wobble, that is a hand problem rather than an eye problem, and it is worth tracing a few first purely to get the movement into your arm. Trace the same eye five times, then draw it freehand and compare — the gap between the two is exactly what you still need to practise.

Styles differ

There is no single anime eye. Shoujo eyes are tall, round and full of catchlights. Shounen eyes are narrower and more angular. Older characters have thinner lash lines and smaller irises; children have the opposite. Pick one style and stay in it for a whole page rather than mixing them.
