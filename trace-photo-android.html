The best way to trace a photo onto paper on Android

What actually matters in a tracing app, and what does not.

There are dozens of tracing apps on Google Play and most of them do the same thing: show your photo on the screen at low opacity, point the camera at your paper, and let you draw. They differ in one respect that matters far more than the feature lists suggest.

The problem with most of them

In a simple tracing app, the picture is stuck to your screen. The camera feed moves behind it, but the image itself does not.

That means the moment your hand brushes the phone — and it will, constantly — the picture is no longer in the same place relative to your paper. You nudge it back, you draw three more lines, it moves again. People give up on tracing apps for this reason and usually blame themselves.

Locking the image helps, because it stops you dragging it by accident. It does not help when the phone itself shifts.

What AR anchoring changes

Phones that support Google's ARCore can work out where surfaces are in the room. An app built on that can attach the picture to the paper rather than to the screen.

Once it is anchored you can lift the phone, move it, put it down at a different angle, and the image is still sitting on the same spot on the page at the same size. You can stop for an hour and come back to it.

Not every phone supports ARCore. A well-built app falls back to the screen-overlay method with a lock button on phones that do not, so it still works everywhere — just less comfortably.

What else actually matters

Line conversion

A photograph is a block of colour and tone. Tracing one directly is difficult because you cannot see your own pencil marks through it. An edge-detection mode that turns the photo into clean contour lines on a transparent background solves this, and it is the feature that separates a usable app from a frustrating one.

Background removal

If you photograph a cat on a sofa, you want to trace the cat. On-device subject segmentation isolates it and drops the background away in one tap.

Opacity control

You need to fade the reference until your own pencil lines are visible underneath. A slider matters more than it sounds.

Screen stays awake

Small, but a drawing session is twenty minutes of barely touching the phone. An app that lets the screen sleep halfway through is unusable in practice.

What does not matter

Template libraries of thousands of images are a selling point, not a feature — you will use four of them. Filters beyond edges, sketch and grayscale are decoration. Anything that asks you to create an account for a drawing tool is wasting your time.

Practical setup

The thing that improves your results most is not the app. It is a phone stand. A £5 clamp that holds the phone steady over the page removes most of the frustration, whatever software you use.

What to check before you install

  1. Does it need an account? It should not.
  2. Does it upload your photos anywhere? Photos should stay on the phone.
  3. Does it work without ARCore, for when you change phones?
  4. Can you convert a photo to lines inside the app, or do you have to prepare it elsewhere first?
