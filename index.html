How to trace a picture onto paper with your phone

No printer, no tracing paper, no light box. Three steps.

1

Put paper down

Any sheet, on a flat surface in decent light. Hold your phone above it or rest it on a stand.

2

Place a picture on it

Choose one of the built-in drawings or import a photo from your gallery. Tap the paper and the picture lands there.

3

Draw what you see

Look at your phone and follow the lines with a pencil. The picture stays on the page while your hand moves.

It stays where you put it

Most tracing apps stick the picture to your screen, so it slides out of position the moment your hand moves. On phones with ARCore, this one pins the picture to the paper itself. Lift the phone, put it down, come back an hour later — the drawing is still in the same place at the same size.

On phones without ARCore it falls back to a screen overlay with a lock button, so it works everywhere.

Learn to draw anime without any talent

Sixteen line drawings are included free: a close-up eye, a pair of eyes, girl and boy faces, a chibi, a magical girl, a samurai, a cat girl, a school girl, a ninja, an angel, a winged dragon, a cat, a sheet of six hands, six hair styles and a full-body proportion guide.

Start with the eye until the lash line feels natural, then move to the face, then the proportion guide. That order teaches you more than copying a finished picture does.

Tools that help you draw

Cut out the background

One tap isolates the subject of your photo and drops the background away, so you trace the thing you care about and nothing else.

Edges mode

Turns a photo into clean contour lines on a transparent background, so you can see your paper through it instead of a flat block of colour.

Sketch and grayscale

A soft pencil rendering, or colour removed entirely so you can read values rather than hues.

Opacity slider

Fade the picture from a faint guide to a solid reference, so your own pencil marks stay visible underneath.

Lock, grid and mirror

Freeze the placement so a stray finger cannot nudge it, overlay rule-of-thirds guides, or flip the reference horizontally.

Torch, and the screen stays awake

Light your paper when your own hand shadows it, and the display never sleeps halfway through a drawing.

Questions

Is tracing cheating?

Artists have argued about this for centuries, and the honest answer is that it depends what you want. Tracing will not teach you to invent a pose from imagination. It will teach you line control, proportion and confidence, and it gets a finished drawing onto the page on a day when you would otherwise have drawn nothing. Most people who draw well started by copying something.

Do I need a special phone?

No. Android 7.0 or newer. Phones that support Google's ARCore get the version that pins the picture to the paper; every other phone gets a screen overlay with a lock button instead. Both work.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes, apart from the one-off download of the background-removal model. Everything else runs on the phone.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Photos you import are copied into the app's own storage on your phone and stay there. The camera feed is never recorded. There is no account and no sign-up.

Is it really free?

Yes. All sixteen templates are included, there is no subscription and there is nothing to buy.

Can children use it?

Yes — it is rated for ages 3 and up. It is a good way to get a child drawing on paper rather than on a screen, since the result is a real drawing they can keep.

What can I trace?

Anything you have a picture of: pets, family photos, cars, flowers, anime characters, logos, a photo you took five minutes ago. Please respect copyright if you intend to publish or sell what you draw.
